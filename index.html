Dragonfruit OS

Ideation Engine — Workflow

What

A Creative Director (CD) connects one client's YouTube channel and that client's knowledge base to a single portal. The engine gathers research from on and off YouTube (weighted to overindex on YouTube-native signals), filters it against who the client is and DFM's published standards, and presents candidate video topics. The CD works each topic until it passes every standard. Each topic that passes gets sample packaging (title + thumbnail) and a client-calibrated predictive score of how many views the video is expected to get. After the video is published, the system compares that prediction to the real view counts at 7 and 28 days and records whether the call was right. Those results feed the next cycle's scoring. That is the self-scoring feedback loop: every prediction is checked against reality, prediction accuracy is itself measured (target: correct 80% of the time), and both the topic selection and the scoring model improve with each cycle. The intended effect is a higher hit rate on what enters production, with research breadth per topic increasing rather than CD hours.

How — the architecture, in three layers

1 · Data layer — the Dragonfruit Data Lake. Storage only; no logic lives here. Contents: the Turso performance database (immutable 7- and 28-day view snapshots, locked targets, and green/red status for every published video, all clients), the per-client benchmarks (derived tables the scoring model reads, signed off by the CD each cycle), and the A/B testing database (packaging split-test outcomes). Client knowledge bases live in the personal layer, not the lake.

2 · Processing layer — the agents. Scheduled and on-demand jobs that read and write the data layer: the research agent compiles queries into research sets; the scoring agent computes predictive view scores against the benchmarks and locked targets; the tracking agent writes view snapshots on schedule; ingestion agents refresh the knowledge-base sources on their cadences. All writes are keyed by client, so concurrent sessions cannot conflict.

3 · Craft layer — the phase brains. A brain is a versioned spec file in the repo. A skill loads its brain at invocation, reads the live standards and the client knowledge base, executes, and writes output back to the working tools. The Ideation Brain is loaded by ideation-phase skills (ideation craft: Adrian's Looms, Content Strategy Grains, outlier methodology, Topic Craft Standards guidance). The Scripting Brain is loaded by scripting-phase skills (YouTube best practices from Adrian Lurie, Paddy Galloway, and Colin & Samir).

Update path. Meetings, Slack, corrections, and the content strategists' monthly report are harvested into a proposals queue. Every change requires human approval before landing: a repo PR for skills, brains, and knowledge; a live-doc edit for the standards ("the bar"), craft guides ("how to hit it"), and client knowledge bases ("who the client is"). Nothing updates itself.

Session scoping: connecting a client binds the session to that client's knowledge base and calibration. The data lake is read-only during ideation. Human gates: the CD approves calibrated lessons and signs off the per-client benchmarks each cycle; only an ECD/CEO approves changes to the Ideation & Craft Standards.

The two-layer principle

The process layer — engine, agents, skills, brains, standards — is the shared product: versioned in the repo, changed only by approved PR. The personal layer — each client's knowledge base, calibration, and lessons — is sealed per client and read live: never shared across sessions and never packaged into the product, enforced in tooling rather than policy. The engine never invents a client fact or the client's voice; client truth comes only from the knowledge base.

Gamified CD portal — design notes

  • Deal (Pull). Each pull deals the CD five primary topic cards, each fanning out into 1–3 angle offshoots with title + thumbnail variants. Weak cards are discarded; standard-qualifying ones that aren't picked persist as Validated topics in the knowledge base.
  • Shape (Present & shape). The CD picks the cards worth working on and adjusts angle, title, and thumbnail concept in a live loop — every DFM standard is a meter that re-scores on each edit, and no topic locks until it meets 100%. The CD makes the final topic selection.
  • Lock (Benchmark & greenlight). The scoring agent delivers the predictive view score from the Per-client benchmarks and the locked target. Clears the bar → the topic locks to the slate, ready to share with the client. Misses → back to Pull with the learnings.
  • The meta-game. Track each CD's calling average — how often their locked topics actually went green at 7 days. Prediction accuracy becomes the CD's score too, not just the model's.
  • Deliberately excluded: leaderboards ranking CDs against each other. Channels differ wildly in difficulty and the multiple absorbs that difference, so the game is always CD-vs-the-bar, never CD-vs-CD.

Engine design rules

  • No comment mining. Comments are under 1% of viewers and a self-selecting group (superfans and haters) — noise, not demand. Engagement rate is an acceptable signal; comment content is not.
  • Expertise-fit is a hard filter. Demand only counts if the client can genuinely deliver on it. Chasing demand the client can't credibly serve produces faked videos that fail — validate against what they're truly expert in.
  • Demand over supply. Don't just clone viral videos. Use outliers to extrapolate the audience's pain points and aspirations, then ideate from those problems — including combining two demand signals into one original concept.
  • Validate the theme, not the video. A single 40× upload is an anecdote; a vein — many videos with sustained views-per-video — is demand evidence. Boolean the theme before greenlighting anything.
  • Comparison-set hygiene. Long-form only, Shorts and boosted excluded, before any multiple is computed. A baseline inflated by Shorts measures the wrong thing.
  • The transcript is the only source of words. Scripts shape the client's interview words — never invent. Hook and close are the only exceptions.
  • The recency rule. A client's new topic suggestion (Slack or the living survey) must surface in the very next ideation round — or get an explicit CD response explaining why not. Silence reads as being ignored.
  • Humans hold the pen. Lessons enter through the CD gate; the CD signs off the Per-client benchmarks each cycle; standards change only through the ECD/CEO gate; brains and skills change only by approved PR.
